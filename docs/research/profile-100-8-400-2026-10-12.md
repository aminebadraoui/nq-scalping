# Profile backtest: $100 risk · cap 8 · +$400 daily target (2026-10-12)

> Question: *"Backtest the profile — $100 risk per trade, 8-micro contract
> cap, stop trading after +$400 on the day."*
> CLI: `web/scripts/sizeDownStudy.ts --budget 100 --cap 8 --target 400`.

## Method

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Instrument / session | NQ 1-min · MNQ ($2/pt) · ETH (Globex) |
| Sample | 2023-12-18 → 2026-10-09 · 654 sessions (641 with ≥ 1 trade) · 5,606 trades |
| Cost model | 0.5 pt round-trip (commissions excluded from sims — engine convention; $0.95/side noted as haircut) |
| Fill model | market-entry replay · pessimistic same-bar exit · stop-first · no-bars dropped |
| Exit model | 1R target / breakout stop (3.5-pt floor) / 60-bar time exit |
| Split | 70/30 by date (train < 2026-01-01 ≤ test) · frozen holdout: — |
| Significance | Wilson 95% CI on WR · P(pass/breach) via 2,000 seeded day-order reshuffles + 10,000-iter different-seed verification |
| Model (if any) | none — the profile picked from the sizing frontier |
| Stack | varies the RISK layer only: signals `MOM_PROD`+`EXP_PROD` · gates default set · fill 0.5 pt |
| CLI | `web/scripts/sizeDownStudy.ts` |

Profile: `contracts = min(8, floor($100 ÷ (risk × $2)))`, floored at 1 (no
trade in the sample exceeds the $100 ceiling); once a day's P&L reaches
+$400, no new trades that day — the trade that crosses it counts, then the
desk goes flat. Compared against the plan baseline ($100/30µ, no arm) and
the same sizing without the arm.

## What this confirms

1. **The profile does exactly what it was chosen for.** P(pass) = 100%,
   P(breach) = 0% in both stages (10k-iter verified), median 13 days
   [10–17] to pass, 9 days to the funded first payout. maxDD $574 = 29%
   of the MLL (green band), worst day −$332 = 17% of the MLL.
2. **The cap-8 carries the drawdown improvement, the arm carries the
   discipline.** Cap-8 alone cuts DD 43% → 29% (vs the 30µ baseline) at
   the cost of 2 days (12 vs 10). The +$400 arm costs 1 more day (13 vs
   12) and 16% of long-run profit ($183k → $154k), and it caps the best
   day at $489 — the 30% consistency gate can never move.
3. **Income still beats the goal.** Mean day $241 net of the arm —
   averaging above the $200/day target while keeping the stop-rule that
   locks in $400 days (153 of 641 days trigger the halt).
4. **Costs:** commissions ≈ $1.90/trade ≈ $14.3/day (~6% of mean day,
   not in the sims) → expect real-world pass ~1 day later than shown.

## The recommended live profile (safety · consistency · reasonable speed)

The user's objective — safety first, consistency, reasonable speed — maps
onto the live day plan with **one change** (the contract cap). Every other
knob below is already the live default:

| Knob | Setting | Why it serves the goal |
|---|---|---|
| `PROP_RISK_PER_TRADE` | $100 (live default) | the frontier's speed sweet spot; no change |
| **contract cap** | **30µ → 8µ** (the one change) | cuts maxDD 43% → 29% of MLL for 2 days of speed — pure safety |
| `PROFIT_ARM` | +$400 one-more-trade (live default) | pins best day ≤ ~$489 → consistency bar stays at $3,000 forever; day sd $256 → $179 (smoother daily P&L) |
| `MFF_CONSISTENCY_CAP` | +$900 (live default) | firm rule; with the arm it never fires, stays as backstop |
| `DAILY_LOSS_FLOOR` | −$250 (live default) | emergency brake; fires 8/641 days, costs nothing |
| give-back + dd600→half | live defaults | give-back smooths green-day reversals; dd600 never triggers at 29% DD |

Expected performance of the stack (from the backtests): **median 13 days
to pass [10–17] · maxDD 29% of MLL · worst day −$332 · P(breach) 0% in
12,000 orderings · P(pass) 100% · mean day $241** — all three viability
bands green except speed's ~13 days, which is still inside the green
band (≤ 20).

The cap change is a live-execution knob: per
`web/EXEC_CHANGE_POLICY.md` it needs the [backtested: sizeDownStudy.ts]
tag and explicit approval before any Coolify/env change — nothing here
changes live behavior on its own. Faster variants exist (drop the arm →
11 days via the $900 cap alone) but trade day-to-day smoothness; the arm
is what buys "consistency" in the daily-income sense.
